How to Photograph Sarees & Ethnic Wear for Online Selling

By CropStudio Textile Studio • Published Aug 2026 • ⏱️ 7 min read

🤖 Key Takeaways (Saree Photography Blueprint)

  • Zari Glare Control: Metallic gold/silver thread (zari) reflects hard light and creates white spots. Use indirect bounced light or a polarizing filter.
  • Pleat Precision: Pin pleats neatly using invisible tape on flat lays to avoid messy folds.
  • Required Angles: Online saree buyers expect 3 standard shots: Full Draped View, Pallu Weaver Detail Close-up, and Blouse Piece Flat Lay.
  • Digital Model Draping: Hiring models for 50+ sarees is cost-prohibitive. Use CropStudio's Saree AI Engine to drape flat lay sarees onto realistic Indian models automatically.

1. Managing Zari Reflection & Silk Shine

Silk sarees, Kanjeevarams, and Banarasis feature intricate gold or silver metallic zari work. Direct studio lights hit these metallic threads, causing camera sensors to overexpose, producing harsh white flare blotches that hide the weave pattern.

Pro Technique: Angle your primary light 45 degrees sideways and bounce it off a white foam board or sheer curtain. Diffused light highlights the sheen of silk without blowing out the golden zari work.

2. Flat Lay Saree Staging vs Model Photoshoots

Flat lay saree photography is quick and cost-effective, but e-commerce buyers convert 3x higher when they can visualize how the saree drapes on an actual person.

Traditionally, booking an Indian fashion model, saree draper, makeup artist, and studio costs between ₹15,000 to ₹40,000 per day for just 15 sarees.
